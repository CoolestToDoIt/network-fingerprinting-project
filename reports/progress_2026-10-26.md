## Milestones

I expanded the historical evaluation with 198 model evaluations, including
Random Forest and logistic regression Tor baselines, exact-feature-group split
checks, duration-feature ablations, and matched VPN application-category tests.
The matched comparison uses the same test flows and equal training row counts
for category-seen and category-unseen models, while removing exact test feature
vectors from both training conditions.

Across seven categories and five splits, Random Forest's category-seen macro-F1
averages 0.902 and its category-unseen macro-F1 averages 0.535. Its unseen-category
score is lower in all 35 matched pairs. VoIP has the largest mean drop, 0.606.
This strengthens the evidence that high within-dataset accuracy does not guarantee
transfer to an application category excluded from training.

The separate Tor Random Forest baseline averages 99.01% accuracy and macro-F1
0.983 on random splits, and 98.90% accuracy and macro-F1 0.981 on feature-group
splits. Those groups contain identical feature vectors, not genuine capture
sessions. I have not treated these results as temporal or application transfer.

I also added per-class metrics, training-majority baselines, independently checked
saved metrics/predictions, documented four frozen models, and prepared a report
draft with figures. Transfer and combined-shift evaluators now exist with tests
that reject unverified compatibility and fit preprocessing only on historical data.
